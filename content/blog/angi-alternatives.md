The main Angi alternatives for contractors and home service pros are Thumbtack, Google Local Services Ads, Yelp, Nextdoor, Bark, Houzz, Facebook, and your own website backed by a strong Google Business Profile. They differ in how you pay, whether leads are shared with competitors, and who owns the customer relationship afterwards.

If you're paying for Angi leads and losing most of them to cheaper competitors, here's how each alternative works.

## Why contractors look for Angi alternatives

Housecall Pro's [explainer on how Angi works](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-angis-list-how-angi-works/) notes that Angi often sends the same lead to multiple pros, cites contractor-reported prices of roughly **$15 to $85 per lead** plus membership fees, and points out that you pay for leads you don't win. We compared it in detail in [Angi vs Thumbtack vs your own website](/blog/angi-vs-thumbtack-vs-your-own-website).

None of that makes Angi useless. It just means it's worth knowing the other options.

## The 8 alternatives at a glance

| Platform | How you pay | Leads shared? | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Local Services Ads | Per lead | No, the customer contacts you | Most home services |
| Your website + Google profile | Your time or a monthly service | No | Every business, long term |
| Thumbtack | Per lead, with a budget | Often | Handyman, cleaning, smaller jobs |
| Yelp | Free page, paid ads | Varies | Cleaning, moving, some trades in cities |
| Nextdoor | Free page, paid Local Deals | No | Neighborhood businesses |
| Bark | Credits per lead | Yes | Testing new services or areas |
| Houzz | Paid software plans | Varies | Remodelers and designers |
| Facebook | Free page, paid ads | No | Visual trades and local groups |

## 1. Google Local Services Ads

Local Services Ads appear at the very top of Google with a Google Verified badge, and they're [moving inside Google Ads](/blog/local-services-ads-moving-to-google-ads) during 2026 and 2027. You [pay for valid leads, not clicks](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en): answered calls, messages, booking requests, and missed calls during business hours where the caller waits more than 20 seconds. Leads Google judges invalid aren't charged, and repeat contacts from the same person within 15 days aren't charged twice.

The big difference from lead sites: the customer calls you, not a list of pros. Our guide to [Local Services Ads missed-call charges](/blog/google-local-services-ads-missed-calls) explains why answering matters so much.

## 2. Your own website and Google Business Profile

The only lead source you own. Calls and forms from your Google listing and website go to you alone, and the cost per lead falls as you rank. It takes months to build, so most businesses run it alongside paid channels. Start with our [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-home-service-businesses).

## 3. Thumbtack

Thumbtack [uses a pay-per-lead model](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-thumbtack-how-it-works/): you're charged when it matches you with a customer or when you choose to contact one. You set a weekly or monthly budget and stop getting leads when you hit it. There's no subscription fee, and no refund if a lead doesn't become a job. Contractors report lead costs from about $10 to over $100 depending on trade and city.

## 4. Yelp

A Yelp business page is free and matters for some trades, especially in cities. Yelp sells ads on top. One thing to know before you start: Yelp's rules tell businesses [not to ask customers for reviews](https://searchengineland.com/yelp-increasingly-cracking-review-solicitation-across-internet-286290) at all, so review building there is slower than on Google.

## 5. Nextdoor

Claiming a Nextdoor business page is [free](https://www.constantcontact.com/blog/nextdoor-advertising/), and recommendations from neighbors drive visibility: a business doesn't appear in Nextdoor search until it has three recommendations. The paid option, Local Deals, targets specific neighborhoods. It suits businesses whose customers are clustered in a few areas.

## 6. Bark

Bark sells credits, and you spend credits to respond to leads. The cost of each lead is shown before you respond, and Bark lists a credit price of [$2.35 per credit](https://help.bark.com/hc/en-us/articles/13346288068892), with leads costing a varying number of credits. Unused credits can't be refunded and, for purchases since November 2025, expire after three months. Useful for testing demand for a new service; watch your cost per booked job closely.

## 7. Houzz

Houzz is built for remodelers, builders and designers, with project photos as the main draw. Houzz Pro is software for estimates, CRM and marketing, with paid plans [listed on Capterra from $399 a month](https://www.capterra.com/p/199689/Houzz-Pro/pricing/) and a free tier. Worth it mostly for design-led, higher-ticket work.

## 8. Facebook

A Facebook page and local community groups are free; ads let you target homeowners in your service area. It works best for visual trades and seasonal offers. Our guide to [Facebook marketing for home service businesses](/blog/facebook-marketing-home-service-business) covers what works now.

## Don't forget referrals

In [Jobber's 2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/), **59%** of home service owners named referrals and repeat customers as a top lead source, against **16%** for lead generation sites. A simple follow-up after each job and a referral thank-you often beat any paid platform.

## How to choose

1. **Track cost per booked job,** not cost per lead. A $20 lead that never books costs more than a $90 lead that does.
2. **Answer fast.** On shared-lead platforms, the first to call usually wins.
3. **Put your own channels first.** Use paid platforms to fill gaps while your website and Google profile build up.
