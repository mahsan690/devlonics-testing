After-hours calls are often the most urgent calls a home service business gets: a burst pipe at 10 p.m., no heat on a Sunday, water coming through the ceiling. If nobody answers, most customers simply call the next company. You have four realistic options: voicemail, forwarding to an on-call technician, a live answering service or an AI receptionist. Here's how they compare.

## What's at stake

A [Service Direct study of 1,000 home service calls](https://blog.servicedirect.com/home-service-call-performance-report) found in-house teams answered **66%** of calls, while call answering services answered **99%**. The business owners in the study thought they answered 97%. The study is from 2019, but the pattern still holds: calls slip through when everyone is busy or off the clock.

Customers expect speed. [Jobber's 2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/) found more than **55%** of home service customers expect a reply within the hour, and **28%** expect an immediate one.

If you run Local Services Ads, unanswered calls during your business hours can also be [charged as leads](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en) when the caller waits more than 20 seconds.

## Option 1: Voicemail

**How it works:** Calls go to a recorded message after hours.

**Good for:** Businesses with no emergency work and customers happy to wait until morning.

**Watch out for:** Many callers hang up without leaving a message. If you rely on voicemail, add an automatic [missed call text back](/blog/missed-call-text-back) and call everyone back first thing.

## Option 2: Forwarding to an on-call technician

**How it works:** After-hours calls ring a rotating on-call tech's phone.

**Good for:** Small teams with genuine emergency work.

**Watch out for:** Techs who miss calls while working, driving or sleeping, and burnout. Calls answered this way also often never make it into your CRM.

## Option 3: A live answering service

**How it works:** Trained agents answer in your business name, follow your script, take details and dispatch emergencies.

**Good for:** Businesses with lots of emotional or complicated calls, or customers who strongly prefer a person.

**Watch out for:** Per-minute or per-call costs that rise in busy seasons, agents who don't know your services well, and messages that arrive as emails someone has to retype.

## Option 4: An AI receptionist

**How it works:** An AI voice assistant answers every call, asks your questions, answers common ones (service areas, pricing ranges, availability), books jobs into open slots and transfers true emergencies to a person.

**Good for:** Businesses with lots of routine calls, high volumes at peak times and calendars that can be booked online.

**Watch out for:** A setup that's too clever. It should be honest that it's an assistant, keep calls short and always give emergencies a path to a human.

## Side by side

| | Voicemail | On-call forwarding | Answering service | AI receptionist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Answers every call | No | Often not | Nearly all | Yes |
| Books jobs | No | Sometimes | Sometimes | Yes, into open slots |
| Handles emergencies | No | Yes | Dispatches | Transfers to a person |
| Cost | Lowest | Staff time | Per minute or call | Monthly plus usage |
| Lead lands in your CRM | No | Rarely | With an integration | Yes, if connected |

## What to set up, whichever you choose

1. **A clear after-hours path for emergencies.** Customers should never have to guess.
2. **Every call logged.** Name, number, address, the problem and what happened, sent to your email or CRM.
3. **A text confirmation to the customer,** so they know someone has their details.
4. **A morning review.** Someone checks every after-hours lead first thing and calls back.
5. **Honest hours on your Google profile.** Don't advertise 24/7 unless calls are really answered.
