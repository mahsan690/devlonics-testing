Most small home service businesses should plan to spend somewhere around 7 to 8% of revenue on marketing, then adjust from there. That figure comes from a long-standing [US Small Business Administration guideline](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120927044340/http://www.sba.gov/community/blogs/how-set-marketing-budget-fits-your-business-goals-and-provides-high-return-investmen): businesses with revenue under $5 million should allocate 7 to 8%, assuming profit margins of 10 to 12%.

It's a rule of thumb, not a law. Here's how to turn it into a budget that fits your business.

## Start with the rule of thumb

The SBA guideline (an archived SBA post, but still the most quoted benchmark) adds two caveats worth keeping:

- The right percentage **varies by industry, size and growth stage.**
- **New businesses often spend more** while they build awareness.

Here's what 7 to 8% looks like at different revenue levels:

| Annual revenue | 7% per year | 8% per year | Roughly per month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $300,000 | $21,000 | $24,000 | $1,750 to $2,000 |
| $750,000 | $52,500 | $60,000 | $4,375 to $5,000 |
| $1,500,000 | $105,000 | $120,000 | $8,750 to $10,000 |
| $3,000,000 | $210,000 | $240,000 | $17,500 to $20,000 |

## When to spend more or less

**Spend toward the higher end, or above it, if:**
- You're new or expanding into new towns
- You're adding a service, such as heat pumps or roofing replacements
- Your margins are healthy and you have capacity to take more work

**Spend toward the lower end if:**
- Most work comes from referrals and repeat customers
- You're already booked out for weeks
- Margins are thin and need fixing first

Referrals matter a lot in this industry: in [Jobber's 2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/), **59%** of home service owners named referrals and repeat customers as a top lead source.

## Split it between owned and rented channels

**Owned channels** keep working when you stop paying:
- Your website and service pages
- Your Google Business Profile
- Reviews and reputation
- Your customer list, for maintenance reminders and repeat work

**Rented channels** stop when the money stops:
- Local Services Ads and Google Ads
- Facebook and Nextdoor ads
- Lead sites like Angi and Thumbtack

A common pattern for an established business: put a solid share into owned channels every month, because their cost per lead falls over time, and use rented channels to fill gaps and scale up in peak season. Newer businesses often lean more on paid channels at first while owned ones build up.

## Measure cost per booked job, not cost per lead

A cheap lead that never books is expensive. For each channel, every month:

1. **Spend:** what you paid for the channel, including fees and management.
2. **Booked jobs:** how many jobs came from it.
3. **Cost per booked job:** spend divided by booked jobs.
4. **Profit per job:** your average job value minus job costs.

Keep spending where cost per booked job is comfortably below profit per job, and fix or cut the rest. This only works if you know where each call and form came from: our guide to [measuring website ROI](/blog/measure-website-roi-call-tracking-ga4) covers call and form tracking.

## Don't forget the leaks

Before adding budget, fix what's losing the leads you already pay for:

- Calls that go unanswered ([missed call text back](/blog/missed-call-text-back) helps)
- Slow replies to forms ([speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses))
- A website that doesn't convert on mobile
- A Google profile with old reviews and no replies

Fixing these is often the cheapest marketing a business can buy.

## A simple monthly budget review

| Question | If the answer is no |
| --- | --- |
| Is every lead source tracked? | Fix tracking before changing budgets |
| Is cost per booked job below profit per job? | Cut or fix that channel |
| Are we answering every call and form fast? | Fix the leak before buying more leads |
| Are reviews coming in every week? | Restart the review routine |
| Is next season's content live? | Publish now, before the peak |
