AI is already part of everyday work for half of home service businesses. The owners getting the most from it use it for repetitive office tasks, like estimates, follow-ups, review replies and call notes, rather than expecting it to run the business. Here are seven practical uses and where to keep a human in charge.

## How many home service businesses use AI?

Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/), a survey of 1,050 US owners, found:

- **52%** use AI in daily operations, and **27%** plan to adopt it within the next year.
- Among current users: **54%** use it for quoting, **52%** for invoicing and **51%** for business writing.
- About **1 in 5** owners don't think AI will help their business.

## 1. Drafting estimates and quotes

Describe the job in plain words and let an AI assistant draft the scope, line items and customer-friendly explanation, using your own price book. You review and adjust the numbers. Many field service apps now build this in.

## 2. Follow-ups that actually happen

Unsent follow-ups are lost jobs. AI can draft a personal follow-up for every open quote ("Hi Dana, just checking whether you had questions about the water heater options"), which you approve and send, or which goes out automatically from your software.

## 3. Replying to reviews

Paste a review and get a draft reply that thanks the customer by name and mentions the job. Edit it so it sounds like you: BrightLocal's 2026 survey found [50% of consumers are put off by generic, templated replies](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/).

## 4. Call and job summaries

Recording apps with AI transcription (with customer consent where required) can turn a call into a summary, a job note and a to-do list, so details don't get lost between the office and the van.

## 5. Answering after-hours calls and messages

An AI receptionist or chat assistant can answer common questions, collect details and book simple jobs when your office is closed. With Google now [charging for missed Local Services Ads calls](/blog/google-local-services-ads-missed-calls), capturing overflow calls is worth more than ever. Test it on real calls and set a clear handoff to a person for emergencies. Here's [how we built one for an HVAC and plumbing company](/work/ai-voice-receptionist-home-services): it answers around the clock, books into the calendar and sends emergencies to the on-call tech.

## 6. Writing for your website and social media

AI can help draft service descriptions, FAQs, job posts and social captions. Use it for structure and a first draft, then add what only you know: real prices, local details and photos of your work. That specific detail is also what helps you show up in [AI search](/blog/ai-overviews-local-search-service-businesses).

## 7. Automating the busywork between tools

The biggest time savings often come from connecting the systems you already have: a form that creates a job in your software, sends a confirmation text and adds the customer to your email list, or a weekly report that pulls jobs, revenue and leads into one place. That's where [automation](/services/automation) earn their keep.

## Where to keep a human in charge

- **Pricing:** AI drafts, you decide.
- **Diagnoses and safety advice:** never let a chatbot tell a customer a gas smell is fine.
- **Anything legal or contractual.**
- **Reviews:** using AI to write fake reviews is explicitly banned by the FTC's rule on consumer reviews.

## Where to start

Pick one task that eats time every day, try AI on it for two weeks, and measure the hours saved. Then pick the next one.
