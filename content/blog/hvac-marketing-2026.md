HVAC marketing in 2026 is less about shouting discounts and more about answering the questions homeowners are nervous to ask: Is my system really at the end of its life? What will a new one cost, and will it pay for itself? Can I still repair it? The companies that explain this clearly, on their website and at the door, win the replacement jobs.

## What's changed for HVAC companies

### Refrigerant rules

Under the EPA's Technology Transitions program, residential and light commercial AC and heat pump equipment using higher-GWP refrigerants and [manufactured before January 1, 2025 could be installed only until January 1, 2026](https://hardinet.org/posts/advocacy/epa-moves-install-date-for-residential-and-light-commercial). Newer systems use lower-GWP refrigerants. Existing systems can still be serviced, which is exactly the kind of nuance homeowners get confused about.

### Cautious homeowners

A [Carrier survey of 1,015 US homeowners](https://www.carrier.com/us/en/news/carrier-finds-over-half-of-us-homeowners-plan-2026-home-improvements.html), published in January 2026, found:

- **55%** are considering upgrades or remodeling this year, and **19%** of those are considering a new HVAC system.
- **26%** don't know how old their HVAC system is; **54%** don't know the average lifespan.
- **82%** would consider a new system if it paid for itself through energy savings within five to ten years.
- **49%** are aware of financial incentives for HVAC upgrades.
- Among homeowners not planning projects, **42%** say they don't have the money.

### Demand for technicians

The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects [about 40,600 openings a year for HVAC technicians](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/heating-air-conditioning-and-refrigeration-mechanics-and-installers.htm) through 2035, so hiring stays competitive too.

## Turn homeowner questions into marketing

The Carrier numbers are a content plan. Build pages and short videos that answer:

1. **"How old is my system, and how long should it last?"** Show customers where to find the age on the unit's label and what typical lifespans look like.
2. **"Repair or replace?"** Walk through the decision with honest criteria: age, repair cost, efficiency, refrigerant.
3. **"What does a new system cost?"** Give ranges and what drives them. Our guide on [showing prices on your website](/blog/should-you-put-prices-on-your-website) covers how.
4. **"Will it pay for itself?"** Explain efficiency ratings and estimated savings in plain numbers.
5. **"What rebates and tax credits apply?"** Link to the programs in your state and utility area.
6. **"How can I pay for it?"** Show financing options clearly.

These answers also help you show up in [AI search results](/blog/ai-overviews-local-search-service-businesses), which favor clear, specific explanations.
