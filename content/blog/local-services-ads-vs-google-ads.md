Local Services Ads and Google Ads search campaigns both put a home service business at the top of Google, but they work very differently. Local Services Ads charge you per lead and show a Google Verified badge above everything else; customers call or message you straight from the ad. Google Ads search campaigns charge per click and send people to your website, with far more control over keywords, ads and pages.

Here's how they compare and when to use each.

## How Local Services Ads work

You set a weekly budget, the services you offer and the areas you cover. Google shows your ad to people searching for those services nearby.

You [pay for valid leads, not clicks](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en). A lead is:

- An answered call, or a callback where you speak to the customer
- A missed call during business hours where the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds
- A text, email or voicemail from the customer
- A booking request

Leads Google judges invalid aren't charged, repeat contact from the same person within 15 days isn't charged again, and charged leads are reassessed for 30 days, with credits for poor-quality ones.

Local Services Ads are also [moving inside Google Ads](/blog/local-services-ads-moving-to-google-ads) as a campaign type during 2026 and 2027, with a single Google Verified badge.

## How Google Ads search campaigns work

You choose keywords ("water heater replacement," "roof repair"), write ads, set bids and choose which page of your website each ad sends people to. You pay when someone clicks, whether or not they call.

That means more work and more control: you decide exactly which services to advertise, can exclude searches you don't want ("DIY," "jobs") and can send each click to a page built to convert it.

## Side by side

| | Local Services Ads | Google Ads search |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You pay for | Leads | Clicks |
| Placement | Very top, with Google Verified badge | Top ads, below Local Services Ads |
| Setup | Verification, licenses, insurance checks | Account, keywords, ads, landing pages, tracking |
| Control | Services, areas, budget | Keywords, ads, bids, schedule, pages |
| Needs a website | No, but it helps | Yes |
| Biggest risk | Paying for leads you miss or can't serve | Paying for clicks that never call |

## When Local Services Ads make sense first

- You're a common home service: plumbing, HVAC, cleaning, roofing, electrical
- You can answer calls during business hours, or have a backup
- You have reviews coming in, since reviews show on the ad
- You want calls quickly without managing keywords

## When to add Google Ads search campaigns

- You want more of one high-value job, like replacements or remodels
- You have strong landing pages with clear calls to action
- Call and form tracking is set up, so you can see cost per booked job
- Local Services Ads have hit a ceiling on volume in your area

## Common mistakes with both

- **Missing calls.** With Local Services Ads you pay for many of them anyway. Our guide to [missed-call charges](/blog/google-local-services-ads-missed-calls) explains the rule, and [missed call text back](/blog/missed-call-text-back) catches what slips through.
- **Sending ad clicks to the homepage.** Each service ad should land on that service's page.
- **No tracking.** Without call and form tracking, you can't compare cost per job. Our [ROI tracking guide](/blog/measure-website-roi-call-tracking-ga4) covers the setup.
- **Too wide an area.** Pay only for the towns you actually want to drive to.

## The short answer

Start with Local Services Ads if you can answer the phone. Add search campaigns for your highest-value services once your pages convert and tracking is in place. Keep building your Google Business Profile and website the whole time: that's the channel that doesn't charge per lead.
