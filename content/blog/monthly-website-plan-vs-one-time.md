A monthly website plan spreads the cost of a website over time and usually bundles hosting, updates and small edits. A one-time build costs more upfront, and you then pay separately for hosting and any care plan. Over two or three years the one-time route is usually cheaper, but the bigger difference isn't price. It's whether you own the website at the end.

Here's how to compare them honestly.

## How the two models work

**Monthly website plan (website as a subscription)**
- Little or no upfront cost
- A fixed monthly fee, often with a minimum term
- Hosting, updates and some edits included
- The provider often keeps ownership of the design, and sometimes the content and domain

**One-time build**
- A fixed price for design and build
- You own the finished site
- Hosting and a domain cost extra (often from about $5 a month for hosting and $10 to $20 a year for a domain, as covered in our [website cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-small-business-website-cost))
- Optional care plan for updates, backups and edits

## Compare the total over three years

Monthly plans look cheap per month. Compare the full term instead. Using example numbers only:

| | Monthly plan example | One-time build example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront | $0 | $3,000 |
| Monthly | $250 | $25 hosting and domain, plus an optional $100 care plan |
| 3-year total | $9,000 | $3,900 without care plan, $7,500 with it |
| Own the site at the end? | Depends on the contract | Yes |

Your numbers will differ. The point is to multiply the monthly fee by the realistic time you'll keep the site, and to compare like with like: what's included, and what you keep.

## The ownership questions that matter most

Before signing either kind of deal, get clear written answers to these:

1. **Who owns the domain name?** It should be registered in your name, in your account.
2. **Who owns the content:** text, photos, reviews pages?
3. **Who owns the design and code?** Can you take the site to another host or developer?
4. **What happens if you cancel?** Do you get a copy of the site, or does it go offline?
5. **Is there a minimum term or cancellation fee?**
6. **Who owns the Google Business Profile, analytics and ad accounts?** Always you.

A monthly plan that leaves you with nothing when you leave isn't a website; it's a lease. That can still be fine for a brand-new business testing the waters, as long as you know it going in.

## When a monthly plan makes sense

- You're just starting and need a professional site with little cash upfront
- You want hosting, updates and edits handled with one bill
- The contract lets you keep your domain and content, with a reasonable exit

## When a one-time build makes more sense

- Your website is a main source of leads and you plan to keep it for years
- You want specific features, like a quote funnel, booking or a CRM connection
- You want to own everything and be free to change providers

## The middle option: one-time build plus a care plan

Most established service businesses do best with a site they own, built once at a fixed price, plus an optional monthly care plan for the boring but important work: updates, security, backups and small edits. You get the convenience of a monthly service without renting your own website.
