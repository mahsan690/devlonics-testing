The fastest way to get more Google reviews is to ask every customer, at the moment they're happiest, with a link that takes them straight to the review form. What's changed is how much customers expect: recent reviews, a high rating and replies from the owner. Here are the 2026 numbers and a routine that works.

## What do customers expect from reviews in 2026?

[BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) of 1,002 US consumers found the bar rising fast:

- **97%** read reviews for local businesses, and **41%** always read them, up from 29% in 2025.
- **74%** only consider reviews from the last three months; **32%** look for reviews from the last two weeks.
- **68%** require four stars or more, and **31%** will only use businesses rated **4.5 or higher**, up from 17%.
- **47%** won't use a business with fewer than **20 reviews**.
- **89%** expect owners to respond, **80%** are more likely to use a business that responds to all reviews, and **50%** are put off by generic, templated replies.

The takeaway: a steady flow of new reviews matters more than a big total from years ago.

## What's allowed, and what isn't?

Google's [rating manipulation policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) prohibits businesses from:

- Offering incentives, "such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services," for reviews.
- "Selectively solicit[ing] positive reviews" or discouraging negative ones, the practice often called review gating.
- Setting staff quotas or asking for reviews with specific content.

Google does allow you to ask customers to share a genuine experience. In the US, the [FTC's rule on consumer reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) also bans buying reviews and paying for reviews of a particular sentiment, with civil penalties for knowing violations.

## A review routine that works

### 1. Get your direct review link

In your Google Business Profile, use the "Ask for reviews" or "Get more reviews" option to copy a short link that opens the review form directly. Every extra tap loses people.

### 2. Ask at the moment of delight

The best time is right after a job goes well: the AC is blowing cold, the house is clean, the leak is fixed. Train every technician or cleaner to ask in person, then send the link by text before they leave the driveway.

### 3. Make the message personal and short

> "Thanks for having us out today, Sarah. If we did a good job, would you leave a quick Google review? It really helps a small business like ours: [link]. — Mike, ABC Heating"

Use the customer's name and the technician's name. Personal requests outperform generic ones.

### 4. Follow up once

If there's no review after three or four days, send one polite reminder. Then stop.

### 5. Ask everyone, not just the happy ones

Asking every customer keeps you within Google's rules, and a few honest four-star reviews make the fives more believable.

### 6. Reply to every review within a day or two

BrightLocal found 19% of consumers now expect a same-day reply. Thank people by name and mention the job. For negative reviews, acknowledge the issue, avoid arguing, and offer to fix it offline.

### 7. Show reviews on your website

Put recent reviews next to your call buttons and quote form, where people decide whether to contact you. Link to your Google profile so visitors can see they're real.

Google lists prominence as one of its three local ranking factors, and review count and rating feed into it.
