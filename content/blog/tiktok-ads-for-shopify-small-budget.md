TikTok ads can work on a small budget, but only after the store is ready to sell. When we relaunched Gladiolaz, a hair and wig brand on Shopify that had stopped selling, we spent the first stretch fixing the store, fulfilment and tracking. Then we launched TikTok ads at $20 a day. In the first 15 days, the store brought in about 10 orders with a $120 average order value.

Here's what we did, in order, and what we'd tell any small Shopify store considering TikTok.

## Where the store started

Gladiolaz had traffic problems, messy product data and no working ad system. Fulfilment was fragile, and nothing was measured. Running ads in that state would have paid TikTok to send people to a store that couldn't convert them or ship reliably.

## Step 1: Fix the store first

- **Product data:** we cleaned up products and variants, set compare-at pricing and fixed the collection structure and on-page SEO.
- **Product pages:** clearer photos and details, so a visitor from a 15-second video could decide quickly.

Our checklist for [a Shopify store that isn't selling](/blog/shopify-store-not-selling) covers the same ground, from shipping clarity to checkout.

## Step 2: Make fulfilment reliable

We connected AutoDS with Amazon and Alibaba suppliers, and fixed a TikTok Shop warehouse setting that was blocking fulfilment. There's no point winning orders you can't ship.

## Step 3: Set up tracking before spending

Every order needed to trace back to its campaign. Without that, a small budget gets spread across ads that look busy but don't sell.

## Step 4: Make native-looking creative

TikTok rewards videos that look like TikTok, not polished TV ads. We produced short AI-assisted video ads built for TikTok and Meta feeds, which let us test several angles quickly: different hooks, product shots and benefits.

## Step 5: Launch small and watch the numbers

We planned a 30-day paid strategy and launched at **$20 a day**. In the first 15 days the store brought in **about 10 orders at a $120 average order value**, roughly four times the ad spend in revenue over that period.

## Lessons for small budgets

1. **The store is the multiplier.** Ads amplify whatever the store already does, good or bad.
2. **Creative beats budget.** On TikTok, a strong hook matters more than spend. Test several videos and keep the winners.
3. **Tracking is non-negotiable.** Judge results on orders and return on ad spend, not views or likes.
4. **Fulfilment is marketing.** Late or failed deliveries kill repeat orders and reviews.
5. **Scale slowly.** Raise budgets on what's proven, in steps.

TikTok's reach is real: Pew Research's 2025 survey found [37% of US adults use TikTok](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/). But reach only turns into revenue when the store, fulfilment and tracking are ready.
