To respond to a negative review, reply within a day or two, thank the person, acknowledge the specific problem without arguing, and give a name and phone number so it can be sorted out privately. Keep it short and calm. You're writing for every future customer who reads it, not just the reviewer.

Here's the pattern we use, Google's own rules and templates for the complaints service businesses get most.

## Why your reply matters more than the review

A single negative review rarely sinks a business. An angry reply, or no reply at all, does far more damage.

[BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found **89%** of consumers expect business owners to respond to reviews, and **50%** are put off by generic replies. People reading your reviews are deciding whether to let you into their home. Your reply shows them how you behave when something goes wrong.

## Google's rules for replies

Google publishes [tips for replying to reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122). The important ones:

- **Be professional and polite,** even when the review isn't.
- **Keep it short and simple.** Long replies get skipped.
- **Make it personal:** address the reviewer by name and respond to what they actually said.
- **Never share private information** or attack the reviewer.
- **Don't use replies to offer deals or promotions.**
- **Apologize sincerely** where it's due, without accepting blame for things outside your control.

Google also checks replies against its content policies, so a reply with personal details or insults may not be published at all.

## The 4-step reply pattern

1. **Thank them** for the feedback. Plainly, without sarcasm.
2. **Acknowledge the specific issue.** Name it, so it's clear you read the review.
3. **Say what you're doing about it,** in one sentence.
4. **Take it offline** with a real name and number.

Sign with your name. "Mike, owner" reads very differently from "Management."

## Templates for common complaints

Adapt these. A template that's obviously a template is the generic reply that half of consumers dislike.

**Late arrival or no-show**

> Hi Sarah, thank you for letting us know, and I'm sorry we were late on Tuesday. You set aside time for us and we didn't respect it. We've changed how we confirm arrival windows so customers get a text if we're running behind. I'd like to make this right: please call me directly on (555) 010-0000. Mike, owner

**Price complaint**

> Hi James, thanks for the feedback. I understand the final price was higher than you expected, and we should have explained the extra work before starting, not after. I'd like to walk you through the invoice. Please call me on (555) 010-0000. Mike, owner

**Quality of the work**

> Hi Linda, I'm sorry the repair didn't hold. That's not the standard we hold ourselves to. Our work is covered, and I'd like to get a technician back out this week at no charge to fix it. Please call our office on (555) 010-0000 and ask for me. Mike, owner

**Rude or unprofessional staff**

> Hi Tom, thank you for telling us. The way you were spoken to isn't acceptable, and I've talked with the team member involved. I'd appreciate the chance to hear more and put things right. Please reach me on (555) 010-0000. Mike, owner

**You can't find a record of the customer**

> Hi Alex, thank you for the review. I can't find a job under this name in our records, and I'd like to understand what happened. Please contact me on (555) 010-0000 so we can look into it. Mike, owner

**A review that mentions safety, damage or legal issues**

> Hi Dana, thank you for raising this. We take it seriously and I'd like to speak with you directly. Please call me on (555) 010-0000. Mike, owner

In that last case, keep the public reply short and say nothing about fault. Details belong in a private conversation.

## What not to do

- **Don't argue the details in public,** even if you're right.
- **Don't reveal information about the job,** the address or the customer.
- **Don't offer refunds or discounts in the reply.** Google advises against promotions in replies, and the offer can look like a bribe.
- **Don't ask friends to bury it with five-star reviews.** The FTC's rule on consumer reviews bans [buying reviews and suppressing negative ones](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials).

## When to report a review instead

Some reviews break Google's rules: spam, a competitor, a former employee, someone who was never a customer, or abusive content. You can [report those from your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773). A genuine bad review usually stays, and that's fine. Google itself notes that a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy.

## After you reply

Fix the underlying problem if it's real, and tell the customer when it's done. If they choose to update their review, great. If not, your reply still does its job for everyone else reading.
