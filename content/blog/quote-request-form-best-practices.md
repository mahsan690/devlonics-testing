A quote request form that converts is short, works perfectly on a phone, asks for contact details early and sends every submission to your team the moment it arrives. Most contractor websites get the first part half right and miss the rest: the lead arrives in an inbox someone checks twice a day, or it never arrives because the visitor gave up on question nine.

Here are the 10 rules we build every quote form around.

## 1. Ask only what you need to call back

Every extra field is another reason to stop. The essentials:

- Name
- Phone number
- Email
- Service needed
- ZIP code or address
- A short description or a photo

Budget, timeline and "how did you hear about us" can wait for the call.

## 2. Split it into short steps

One question per screen, starting with easy ones ("What do you need help with?"), feels quicker than one long page, especially on a phone. Show progress so people know how close they are.

## 3. Ask for contact details early

If someone stops halfway, you still want a way to reach them. When we [rebuilt Florida Surface Pro's quote funnel](/work/florida-surface-pro), we moved name, phone and email ahead of the address. Now anyone who stops partway through is sent to the team ten minutes later, unless they finish first.

## 4. Design for phones first

Most people fill in your form on a phone. Big buttons, the right keyboard for each field (number pad for phone and ZIP), autofill and nothing that needs pinching or zooming.

## 5. Let people choose a preferred time

A "preferred day and time" step tells your office when to call and makes the customer feel booked, even if you confirm by phone. Show only the days and hours you can actually offer. Full online booking works best for standard jobs; our guide to [online booking for home services](/blog/online-booking-for-home-service-businesses) compares the two.

## 6. Accept photos

For many jobs, a photo answers half your questions before the call: the leak, the roof damage, the floor. Make it optional, but offer it.

## 7. Alert your team instantly

[Jobber's 2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/) found more than **55%** of home service customers expect a reply within the hour, and **28%** expect an immediate one. Send every submission to the phone of whoever calls back, not just a shared inbox. Our guide to [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) explains why minutes matter.

## 8. Send it straight to your CRM

Retyping leads from email wastes time and loses details, especially where the lead came from. Connect the form to your CRM or job software so each request becomes a customer record with the service, preferred time and source attached. Florida Surface Pro's leads land in their CRM, Builder Prime, within seconds. Our guide to [website-to-CRM lead flows](/blog/website-to-crm-lead-flow) covers how.

## 9. Make the thank-you page useful

Don't end on "Thanks, we'll be in touch." Say exactly what happens next and when: "Sarah will call you within the hour during business hours." Add a few reviews, your phone number for urgent jobs, and a text confirmation so they don't keep shopping around.

## 10. Track every submission to its source

Save which ad, page or search brought each lead, so you can see which marketing produces booked jobs, not just form fills. Our guide to [measuring website ROI](/blog/measure-website-roi-call-tracking-ga4) covers call and form tracking.
