Homeowners planning a $50,000 remodel don't hire the first contractor they find. They research for weeks, compare portfolios, read reviews and try to understand how the project will go. A contractor website that wins bigger jobs gives them proof: detailed projects like theirs, a clear process and an honest sense of cost.

## Demand is there, and buyers are careful

A [Carrier survey of 1,015 homeowners](https://www.carrier.com/us/en/news/carrier-finds-over-half-of-us-homeowners-plan-2026-home-improvements.html), published in January 2026, found **55%** were considering upgrades or remodeling this year, and **89%** were likely to prioritize upgrades that offer long-term value. Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/) found customers weigh **price (62%)** and **reviews (51%)** most when choosing a provider, and that **quality of workmanship (69%)** matters most once the job starts.

So homeowners are spending, but they want to see quality, understand value and trust you before signing.

## 1. Replace the gallery with project pages

A grid of photos says "we do work." A project page says "we did this exact kind of work, and here's how it went." For each featured project:

- **The brief:** what the homeowner wanted and any constraints.
- **Before and after photos,** plus a few in-progress shots.
- **Scope, timeline and budget range.**
- **Challenges and how you solved them:** structural surprises, permits, tight access.
- **A quote from the client,** with permission.

Lead with the projects that match the jobs you want more of.

## 2. Explain your process

Big projects feel risky. Walk through what happens from first call to final walkthrough: consultation, design, estimate, contract, permits, scheduling, communication during the build, cleanup and warranty. Clear process copy answers half the questions before the first meeting.

## 3. Publish price ranges

"Most kitchen remodels we complete land between $40,000 and $85,000, depending on layout changes, cabinetry and finishes." A range like this filters out small jobs and reassures serious buyers. Our guide to [showing prices on your website](/blog/should-you-put-prices-on-your-website) covers how, and [offering financing](/blog/offer-financing-on-your-website) can help bigger projects go ahead.

## 4. Show you're established and safe

- License number and insurance.
- Years in business, team photos and your local address or service area.
- Warranties on workmanship.
- Manufacturer certifications and trade association memberships.

## 5. Keep reviews recent and visible

Put reviews next to your consultation button and on project pages. Ask clients for reviews at the final walkthrough, while they're thrilled with the result; our guide on [getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) has the routine.

## 6. Make the consultation request easy

A short form with project type, rough budget, timeline and photo upload. Respond within a business day with next steps. Remodeling buyers notice how you communicate before the contract.
