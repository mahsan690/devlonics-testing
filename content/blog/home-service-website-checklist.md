A home service website has one job: get the phone to ring. Not win design awards, not explain your company history. Homeowners with a leak, a broken AC or an overgrown yard decide in seconds whether to call you or hit the back button.

This checklist covers the 15 things that make the difference, grouped by where they sit on the site. It applies to HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, cleaning, landscaping, pest control and most other trades.

## The first screen: what visitors see before they scroll

### 1. A tap-to-call button on every page

On a phone, your number should be one tap away at the top of every page, and ideally in a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen. If someone has to hunt for it, they'll call a competitor whose number they can see.

### 2. What you do and where you do it, in one line

"Emergency plumbing in Edison and Middlesex County" tells a visitor in two seconds whether they're in the right place. "Quality you can trust" doesn't.

### 3. One clear next step

Pick the main action, usually calling or requesting a quote, and make it the obvious button. A second option, such as booking online, is fine. Five competing buttons are not.

### 4. Proof right next to the button

A star rating, your review count, "licensed and insured" and a guarantee belong beside the call button, where the decision happens, not buried on an about page.

## The pages: what your site needs to contain

### 5. A page for every service

Drain cleaning and water heater replacement are different searches from different customers. A dedicated page for each lets it rank on its own and lets you answer that customer's specific questions.

### 6. A page for every main town you serve

People search for a trade plus their town. Google's own guidance lists [relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) as the main local ranking factors, and a page about your work in a specific town is one of the clearest relevance signals you can send. Write each one with real local detail: neighborhoods, typical jobs and reviews from customers there. Pages with only the town name swapped don't help anyone.

### 7. A reviews page, and reviews everywhere

Reviews are the single biggest trust signal for local businesses. In [BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) of 1,002 US consumers:

- **97%** read reviews for local businesses.
- **74%** only care about reviews written in the last three months.
- **47%** won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.
- **80%** are likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews.

Show your best reviews on every service page, and link to your Google Business Profile so visitors can see the full picture.

### 8. A gallery of real work

For roofing, remodeling, landscaping and anything visual, before-and-after photos sell more than any headline. Tag each project with its town. Stock photos of smiling models in hard hats do the opposite.

### 9. An honest about page

Homeowners are letting you into their house. A real photo of you and your team, how long you've been in business, your licenses and what you stand for all lower the risk of calling.

### 10. Answers to the questions people ask

Do you charge for estimates? Are you available on weekends? Do you offer financing? A short FAQ on each service page answers objections before they stop a call, and it gives search engines and AI assistants clear answers to quote.

## Conversion: making it easy to get in touch

### 11. Short forms

Name, phone number and what they need. Every extra field costs enquiries. For non-urgent jobs, a photo upload field lets customers show the problem, which helps you quote faster.

### 12. Online booking where it fits

If a job can be scheduled without a site visit, such as a cleaning, a tune-up or a weekly mow, let customers book online. It captures the people browsing at 10pm who would never call.

### 13. Pricing guidance

You don't need a full price list. Starting prices for common jobs, or an instant estimate for cleaning, set expectations and filter out bad-fit leads. Price is usually the first question; answering it earns trust.

## Technical: the parts visitors never see

### 14. Fast and built for phones

Most home service visitors arrive from Google on a phone, often stressed and in a hurry. The site should load quickly over mobile data, with text readable without zooming and buttons big enough to tap.

### 15. Call and form tracking from day one

Set up tracking for calls, form submissions and bookings before launch, in Google Analytics 4 or your call tracking tool. Without it, you can't tell which pages, searches or ads bring in jobs, and every marketing decision becomes a guess.

## A quick self-check

Open your website on your phone and ask:

- Can I call in one tap from every page?
- Does the first screen say what we do and where?
- Is there a page for each service and each main town?
- Are recent reviews visible next to the call button?
- Does the quote form have five fields or fewer?
- Do I know how many calls the site brought in last month?

If you answered no to two or more, your website is probably costing you jobs.
