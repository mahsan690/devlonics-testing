Home care agency marketing has two audiences: families looking for trustworthy care for someone they love, and caregivers deciding where to work. Both research carefully before they reach out. A website that answers their trust questions clearly, backed by recent reviews and a fast, human response, does more than any slogan.

## How families choose home care

The decision is usually made by an adult child, often in a hurry after a hospital stay or a fall, and often while working. They want to know:

- Is this agency licensed, insured and reputable?
- How are caregivers screened and trained?
- How do you match a caregiver to my parent?
- What does it cost, and what's covered?
- How quickly can care start?

If your website doesn't answer these, they'll call an agency whose website does.

## Build a website that earns trust

- **Services in plain language:** personal care, companionship, respite, dementia care, post-hospital care, with what each includes.
- **Screening and training:** background checks, training hours, supervision and how you handle a caregiver who isn't the right fit.
- **How matching works,** step by step.
- **Costs:** typical hourly rates or minimums, and which payment sources you accept.
- **Licensing and insurance,** stated clearly.
- **Real reviews and family stories,** with permission.
- **A gentle call to action:** "Talk to a care coordinator" with phone, form and response times.
- **Accessibility:** many visitors are older adults. Readable text, strong contrast and keyboard-friendly navigation matter; our guide to [website accessibility](/blog/ada-website-compliance-small-business) has a checklist.

If your agency is a HIPAA-covered entity, talk to your compliance advisor about what health information your website forms collect and how it's handled. When in doubt, ask for contact details only and discuss care needs by phone.

## Reviews and local search

Families check reviews before they call. BrightLocal's [2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found **74%** of consumers only consider reviews from the last three months and **89%** expect owners to respond. Ask satisfied families for reviews at natural moments, and reply to every review with care, without sharing any client details.

## Recruiting caregivers is marketing too

Agencies win or lose on staffing. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects [18% growth for home health and personal care aides from 2025 to 2035, with about 760,500 openings a year](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/home-health-aides-and-personal-care-aides.htm). Turnover is high: figures commonly cited for home care include about **75%** from PHI and **79.2%** from Activated Insights' 2024 benchmarking report, [as summarized by EngineHire](https://enginehire.io/caregiver-retention-and-turnover-for-home-care-2026/).

A strong careers page helps: pay ranges, schedules, training, real caregiver stories and an application that takes two minutes on a phone. Our guide to [building a careers page](/blog/careers-page-hiring-technicians) applies just as well to caregivers.

## Referral sources still matter

Hospital discharge planners, social workers, senior living communities and physicians refer families every week. A professional website with a clear referral page makes it easier for them to recommend you.
