Yes, most home service businesses should show some pricing on their website, just not necessarily a full price list. Starting prices, typical ranges and clear service or trip fees answer the first question every customer has, without locking you into numbers for jobs that vary. Customers rank price above everything else when choosing who to call.

## How much does price matter to customers?

Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/), based on a survey of 1,050 US owners in December 2025, found customers choosing a provider weigh:

- **Price: 62%**
- **Reviews: 51%**
- **Response speed: 25%**

The same report found **65%** of businesses raised prices in the past year, mostly because of inflation and labor costs, yet only **55%** of owners say they're very confident in their pricing, and just **16%** offer tiered good, better, best options.

## Why hiding prices costs you calls

When a page says only "call for a quote," many visitors assume the price is high or will be a fight. They go back to Google and find someone who gives them a number. Showing a range does the opposite: it filters out people who can't afford you and reassures the ones who can.

## Four ways to show prices without boxing yourself in

### 1. Exact prices for standard jobs

Tune-ups, inspections, standard cleanings, drain clearing and diagnostic visits are predictable. List them plainly.

### 2. "Starting at" prices

"Water heater installation from $1,450" sets an anchor and lets you explain what moves it up: size, fuel type, venting, code upgrades.

### 3. Typical ranges with the reasons behind them

"Most bathroom remodels we do land between $14,000 and $28,000, depending on size, layout changes and finishes." A short list of what drives the price is genuinely useful, and it's the kind of content AI search tools quote.

### 4. Clear fees and policies

Diagnostic or trip fees, whether they're credited against the repair, after-hours rates and payment options. Surprises cause bad reviews; stated fees don't.

## Try good, better, best

With only 16% of businesses offering tiered pricing, three clear options can set you apart:

| Option | Example (AC not cooling) |
| --- | --- |
| Good | Repair the failed part |
| Better | Repair plus a yearly maintenance plan |
| Best | High-efficiency replacement with financing |

Showing options on your website, even as examples, signals that you'll give customers a choice rather than a single take-it-or-leave-it price.

## When a quote really is better

Large or highly variable jobs, such as full remodels, roofing replacements or custom installs, still need a site visit. Even then, show a typical range and explain your quoting process so customers know what happens next and how long it takes.

## Put pricing where it helps the most

- On each **service page**, near the call button.
- In a short **FAQ** ("How much does a furnace tune-up cost?").
- In your **Google Business Profile** services list.

That FAQ is worth doing anyway: Jobber found **74%** of home service businesses have no FAQ section on their site at all.
