A service-area business is one that goes to its customers: plumbers, HVAC techs, cleaners, roofers, landscapers. If customers don't come to your address, Google's guidance is simple: [remove your address from your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) and list the areas you serve instead.

Here's how to set it up properly, and the mistakes that cause problems later.

## Storefront, service-area or hybrid?

| Type | Example | What Google should show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Storefront | A showroom customers visit | Your address |
| Service-area business | A plumber who only works at customers' homes | Your service areas, no address |
| Hybrid | A remodeler with a showroom who also works on-site | Your address and your service areas |

If customers really do visit you during posted hours, you're a hybrid and can show both. If they don't, remove the address.

## Google's rules for service areas

From Google's [service area help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en):

- You can set **up to 20 service areas,** using cities, postal codes or other areas.
- You **can't set a radius** around your business.
- The boundaries of your overall area **shouldn't be more than about 2 hours of driving time** from where your business is based.
- If you don't serve customers at your address, **remove the address.**

You still need a real business address to create and verify the profile. It just isn't shown publicly.

## How to choose your 20 areas

1. **Start with where you actually work,** using your last year of jobs.
2. **Prioritize profitable areas,** not just the biggest city nearby.
3. **Use the names customers use:** the city or neighborhood they'd type in.
4. **Stay inside your real travel range.** Listing places you won't drive to brings calls you'll turn down.

## Common mistakes

- **Showing your home address** when customers never visit it.
- **Using a PO box, mailbox or an address you don't really work from** to get a "better" location. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) don't accept them, and this is a common reason for suspensions.
- **Listing a whole region or every town for 100 miles.** It doesn't make you rank there, and it can look spammy.
- **Creating extra profiles for each town** you serve. One business, one location, one profile.
- **Changing the address and areas frequently,** which can trigger re-verification.

If your profile has already been suspended, our guide to [Google Business Profile suspensions](/blog/google-business-profile-suspended) explains how to recover.

## Service areas don't replace website pages

Setting "Plano" as a service area tells Google and customers you work there. It doesn't give Google much reason to rank you there over a company based in Plano.

That's what service-area pages on your website are for: a page for each main town with real local content, such as neighborhoods, typical jobs and reviews from local customers. Our guide to [service area pages that rank](/blog/service-area-pages-seo) covers how to write them without thin, copied pages.

The profile and the website work together:

| Where | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Profile service areas | Tells customers and Google where you travel |
| Profile categories and services | Tells Google what you do |
| Website town pages | Gives Google local relevance and customers local proof |
| Local reviews | Builds prominence and trust in each area |

## Quick setup checklist

- [ ] Address removed (if customers don't visit)
- [ ] Up to 20 service areas, all within your real range
- [ ] Specific [primary category](/blog/google-business-profile-categories) and complete services list
- [ ] Accurate hours and phone number
- [ ] A website page for each main town
- [ ] Reviews coming in from customers across your area
