You can measure your website's return on investment with three numbers: how many leads it produces, how many of those become jobs, and what those jobs are worth. Most service businesses don't know the first number, because calls aren't tracked and form leads get mixed in with everything else. Here's how to set up measurement properly and report it in one page a month.

## The simple ROI formula

> **Website ROI = (revenue from website leads − cost of the website and its marketing) ÷ cost**

To fill it in, you need:

1. **Leads from the website:** calls, form submissions, bookings and chat messages.
2. **Close rate:** the share of those leads that become paying jobs.
3. **Average job value,** or better, customer lifetime value for recurring services.
4. **Costs:** the website itself, hosting, SEO, ads and tools.

## Step 1: Track forms and bookings in GA4

In Google Analytics 4, mark the actions that matter as **key events**: form submissions, booking confirmations and clicks on your phone number or email. As [LovesData explains](https://www.lovesdata.com/blog/google-analytics-key-events/), you can turn an existing event into a key event in the Admin area by clicking the star next to it in the events list.

Test each one: submit your own form and check it appears in GA4's real-time report.

## Step 2: Track phone calls

For home service businesses, most leads still call. Two levels of call tracking:

- **Click-to-call tracking:** a GA4 event when someone taps your number on mobile. Free and simple, but it misses people who dial manually.
- **Dynamic call tracking:** a call tracking service shows a different number depending on where the visitor came from (Google organic, Google Ads, Facebook) and forwards to your real line. It records the call source, duration and often a recording.

Keep your main number consistent on your Google Business Profile and directories; dynamic numbers belong on the website only, so your business details stay consistent across listings.

## Step 3: Tag your campaigns

Add UTM parameters to links in emails, social posts and ads (`?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=spring-tuneup`) so GA4 can attribute leads to them. Your Google Business Profile website link deserves a UTM too, so you can separate Maps traffic from regular search.

## Step 4: Connect leads to jobs

The most useful number is cost per booked job, not cost per lead. Ask "How did you hear about us?" on every call, and record the lead source in your field service or CRM software, so you can match leads to revenue. ## Common mistakes

- **Counting traffic instead of leads.** Visitors don't pay invoices.
- **Missing calls entirely,** which hides the website's biggest contribution.
- **Judging too early.** SEO compounds; compare quarters, not weeks.
- **Paying for ads without tracking.** With Google now [charging for missed Local Services Ads calls](/blog/google-local-services-ads-missed-calls), call tracking also shows how many calls you're paying for but not answering.
