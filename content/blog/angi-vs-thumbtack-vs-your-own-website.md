Lead apps like Angi and Thumbtack can fill your calendar quickly, but you're renting customers: leads are often shared with other pros, you pay whether or not you win the job, and the relationship belongs to the platform. Your own website and Google Business Profile take longer to build, but every lead is exclusive, and the cost per lead falls as you rank.

## How lead apps work

Housecall Pro's [explainer on how Angi works](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-angis-list-how-angi-works/) notes that "Angi often sends the same lead to multiple pros," so you're usually competing for the job. Contractor-reported costs it cites:

- **Angi leads:** about **$15 to $85 per lead**
- **Membership:** about **$300 per year**
- **Angi Ads:** **$300+ per month**

It adds that Angi doesn't publish standard pricing, that you pay for leads you don't win, and that prices can rise in peak seasons and busy areas. Its example: five $85 leads that close one $400 job is a loss.

## Where home service businesses actually get leads

Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/) asked 1,050 US owners about their lead sources:

| Lead source | Share of businesses |
| --- | --- |
| Referrals and repeat customers | 59% |
| Facebook | 32% |
| Local networking and partnerships | 25% |
| Google Search | 20% |
| Local Services Ads | 19% |
| Lead generation sites | 16% |

Referrals still dominate. The businesses that grow fastest turn those referrals and their reputation into search visibility, so new customers find them directly.

## When lead apps make sense

- You're new and need jobs while your website and reviews grow.
- You want to test demand in a new service or area.
- The job value is high enough that a low close rate still pays.

If you use them, track cost per booked job, not cost per lead, and respond in minutes: shared leads go to whoever calls first. Our guide to [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) covers how to set that up.

## How to shift toward leads you own

1. **Make your website a lead machine:** tap-to-call, short forms, reviews beside every button, and a page for every service and main town.
2. **Complete your Google Business Profile** and get a steady flow of recent reviews. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews).
3. **Turn customers into referrals:** a follow-up text after every job, an easy way to share your contact, and a thank-you for referrals.
4. **Track every lead by source** so you know when your own channels can replace paid ones.
