Seasonality is the single biggest growth problem home service owners report: a third of them name weather and seasons as a top challenge, ahead of competition and labor. The businesses that handle it best plan their slow months the way they plan their busy ones, with maintenance plans, pre-booking, off-season services and marketing that changes with the calendar.

## How big a problem is seasonality?

In Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/), a survey of 1,050 US owners, the top challenges limiting growth were:

| Challenge | Share of owners |
| --- | --- |
| Weather and seasonality | 34% |
| Competition | 28% |
| Labor shortages | 23% |
| Pricing and margins | 20% |
| Cash flow | 17% |

Seasonality and cash flow are linked: a slow quarter is when payroll and van payments hurt most.

## 1. Sell maintenance plans

Maintenance plans turn a one-time repair into scheduled work you control:

- **HVAC:** spring and fall tune-ups, priority service and repair discounts.
- **Plumbing:** annual water heater flushes and inspections.
- **Landscaping:** monthly maintenance, seasonal cleanups and aeration.
- **Cleaning:** recurring weekly or biweekly visits.

The key is scheduling plan visits into your naturally quiet weeks. Promote plans on every service page, at the end of every job and in follow-up emails.

## 2. Market to past customers first

Your customer list is your cheapest marketing channel. A month before your slow season, send a short, specific offer by email and text:

- "Book your furnace tune-up before the first cold snap."
- "Spring cleanup slots for March are open."
- "Get your gutters cleared before the fall rains."

## 3. Open booking early for the next busy season

Run a pre-season booking campaign in the quiet months for work you'll do when demand returns: AC checks in March, landscape design consultations in January, roof inspections before storm season. You fill the busy-season calendar early and get cash flow now if you take deposits.

## 4. Add services that fit the quiet months

Look at what customers ask for when your main service is slow:

- HVAC: indoor air quality, duct cleaning, thermostats.
- Landscaping: holiday lighting, snow removal, tree work.
- Roofing: gutters, attic insulation, inspections.
- Cleaning: move-in and move-out cleans, post-holiday deep cleans.

Give each one a page on your website so people searching for it can find you.

## 5. Change your website with the seasons

- Swap the homepage hero and offers to match what customers need next.
- Publish seasonal guides a month or two before demand peaks, so they're indexed in time.
- Post seasonal updates and offers on your Google Business Profile.
- If you close for part of the year, Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say seasonal businesses should mark the profile temporarily closed during the off-season.

## 6. Keep SEO running when ads pause

Pausing ads in a quiet month can make sense, but keep up reviews, profile posts and website content. SEO compounds: the area pages and guides you publish in the slow season rank in time for the busy one.
