Google Business Profile categories tell Google what your business is, and Google says plainly that [the categories you select affect your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669). For a home service business, the right primary category is one of the most important settings on your profile: it decides which searches you can appear for in the map.

Here's how to choose, with common choices by trade.

## Primary vs additional categories

- **Primary category:** The one that best describes your main business. It carries the most weight and shows on your profile.
- **Additional categories:** Extra categories for other work you genuinely do. They help you appear for related searches.

Google's rules, from its [category help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669):

- Choose the **most specific** option. Its example: "Nail salon," not "Salon."
- If nothing specific fits, choose a **general** category.
- You **can't create your own** category.
- **Don't select a category for every product or service.** Add only a few.
- Changing categories **may trigger re-verification.**

## How to choose your primary category

1. **Describe your business in two words.** "Roofing contractor." "House cleaning service." That's usually your primary category.
2. **Check what Google offers.** Type a few words into the category box in your profile and look at every option. The list changes over time and differs by country.
3. **Look at who ranks.** Search your main service in your town and check the categories shown on the top map results. If all three use the same primary category, that's a strong signal.
4. **Choose the one that matches most of your revenue,** not your most hopeful side service.

## Common categories by trade

These are categories home service businesses often use. Always confirm the exact wording in your profile, since Google's list changes.

| Trade | Common primary category | Additional categories to consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plumbing | Plumber | Drainage service, water heater work you genuinely offer |
| HVAC | HVAC contractor | Air conditioning contractor, heating contractor, furnace repair service |
| Roofing | Roofing contractor | Gutter cleaning service, siding work if you do it |
| Electrical | Electrician | Lighting or EV charger installation, if offered |
| Cleaning | House cleaning service | Commercial cleaning service, carpet cleaning service, janitorial service |
| Landscaping | Landscaper | Lawn care service, landscape designer |
| Remodeling | General contractor or remodeler | Kitchen remodeler, bathroom remodeler |
| Restoration | Water damage restoration service | Mold remediation, fire damage restoration |

## Common category mistakes

- **Too many categories.** Ten loosely related categories dilute what Google thinks you are. Google explicitly advises against it.
- **A general category when a specific one exists.** "Contractor" is weaker than "Roofing contractor" for roofing searches.
- **Categories for departments that operate separately.** Google says independently run, public-facing departments should have [their own profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669), and you shouldn't add their categories to yours.
- **Categories for work you don't do.** It brings calls you can't serve, unhappy reviews and, sometimes, suspension risk.
- **Keyword stuffing elsewhere to compensate.** Adding services or towns to your business name breaks Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

## Categories, services and your website work together

Categories tell Google what you are. The **services list** on your profile says what you do, in detail. Your **website** proves it, with a page for each main service and area.

If you want to rank for "water heater replacement," you need all three: a relevant category, the service listed on your profile and a page on your website that explains it. ## How to change categories safely

1. Write down your current categories and your profile's calls, direction requests and website clicks for the last month.
2. Make one change at a time.
3. Expect that Google may ask you to verify again.
4. Watch the same numbers for the next four to six weeks before changing anything else.
