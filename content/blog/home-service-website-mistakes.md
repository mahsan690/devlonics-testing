Most home service websites are missing at least one of three basics: the areas they serve, their business hours and an FAQ. Jobber's 2026 survey of 1,050 owners found 43% don't list service areas, 65% don't show hours and 74% have no FAQ. Those are exactly what a homeowner checks before deciding to call, and fixing them takes an afternoon.

## The numbers

From Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/):

| Missing from the website | Share of businesses |
| --- | --- |
| Clear service areas | 43% |
| Business hours | 65% |
| An FAQ section | 74% |

## 1. Service areas

**Why it matters:** the first question a homeowner has is "do they come to me?" If the answer isn't obvious, many won't call to find out. Google and AI assistants also use your stated service area to decide when to show you.

**How to fix it:**

- Add a short line near the top of the homepage: "Serving Plano, Frisco, Allen and McKinney."
- Repeat it on every service page and in the footer.
- Create pages for your main towns with genuinely local details, like typical jobs, neighborhoods and local reviews. Avoid near-identical copies with the city name swapped: Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) call pages "targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" doorway abuse.
- Match the list to the service area in your Google Business Profile.

## 2. Business hours

**Why it matters:** customers want to know whether a call will reach someone now. Missing hours make a business feel absent, and they matter more than ever: Google now [charges Local Services Ads advertisers for missed calls](/blog/google-local-services-ads-missed-calls) during listed hours.

**How to fix it:**

- Show office hours and, separately, emergency availability if you offer it.
- Put them in the footer and on the contact page.
- Keep them identical to your Google Business Profile, including holiday hours.
- Only advertise 24/7 if someone actually answers at 2 a.m.

## 3. An FAQ section

**Why it matters:** the same ten questions come up on every call. Answering them on the site saves time, builds trust and gives Google and AI tools clear answers to quote, which matters as [AI search grows](/blog/ai-overviews-local-search-service-businesses).

**How to fix it:** answer the questions customers actually ask, in two or three sentences each:

1. How much does it cost? (ranges and fees)
2. Do you serve my area?
3. How soon can you come out?
4. Are you licensed and insured?
5. Do you offer a warranty?
6. What payment and financing options do you have?
7. What happens during the visit?
8. Do you offer emergency service?

Put a short FAQ on each service page with questions specific to that service, plus a general FAQ page.

## A five-minute self-check

Open your website on your phone and ask:

- [ ] Can I tell where they work within five seconds?
- [ ] Can I see when they're open?
- [ ] Can I call with one tap?
- [ ] Are my top three questions answered?
- [ ] Are there recent reviews near the call button?
