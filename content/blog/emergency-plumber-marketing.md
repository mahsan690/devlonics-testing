Emergency plumbing calls go to the first plumber who answers and seems trustworthy. Someone with water pouring through the ceiling isn't comparing websites; they're calling down the list until a human picks up. So emergency plumber marketing is mostly about being visible at the right moment and answering every single call.

## How emergency customers behave

A burst pipe or overflowing toilet creates a different customer from someone planning a bathroom remodel:

- They search on a phone, often with wet hands, often at night.
- They call the first credible result, not the cheapest.
- If nobody answers, they call the next one immediately.

Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/) found **28%** of home service customers expect an immediate reply. For emergencies, that's everyone.

## The answering problem

In Service Direct's call performance study, [in-house teams answered about 66% of calls, versus 99% for businesses using an answering service](https://www.abby.com/blog/are-your-home-service-calls-performing-well/). For emergency work, every unanswered call is a job handed to a competitor, and since October 2026, Google also [charges Local Services Ads advertisers for missed calls](/blog/google-local-services-ads-missed-calls) during listed hours.

**Fixes:**

- An answering service or on-call rotation that covers nights and weekends.
- Overflow routing so a second phone rings when the first is busy.
- Instant text-back for any missed call: "We got your call. Is it an emergency? Reply YES and we'll call right back."

## Be honest about your hours

Only advertise 24/7 if someone really answers and can dispatch at night. Show office hours and emergency availability separately on your website and Google Business Profile, and keep them consistent. Unstaffed 24/7 hours are also one of the triggers that can get a profile flagged; our guide to [Google Business Profile suspensions](/blog/google-business-profile-suspended) covers the rest.

## Build a site for panicked visitors

- **Phone number at the top** of every page, and a call bar fixed to the bottom of the screen on mobile.
- **"Available now"** messaging when you are, and typical arrival times.
- **What to do while you wait:** how to shut off the main water valve or the water heater. It's genuinely helpful, and it's the kind of answer search engines and [AI tools](/blog/ai-overviews-local-search-service-businesses) surface.
- **After-hours pricing or fees,** stated plainly. Surprise charges at 2 a.m. become one-star reviews.
- **License, insurance and recent reviews** right beside the call button.
- **A fast page:** a heavy site on mobile data loses people before it loads.

## Get found for emergency searches

- **Google Business Profile:** the right primary category, emergency services listed, accurate hours, and recent reviews that mention fast response.
- **An emergency plumbing page** on your website, plus pages for burst pipes, water heater failures, sewer backups and drain emergencies.
- **Local Services Ads and search ads** scheduled for the hours you can actually answer, measured by booked jobs.

## After the emergency

Emergency customers can become long-term customers. Follow up the next day, ask for a review while the relief is fresh (see [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews)), and offer a maintenance plan or inspection.
