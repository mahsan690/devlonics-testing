When someone searches for "emergency plumber" or "AC repair near me," Google shows a map with three businesses before any regular results. That map is where most home service calls come from, and local SEO is the work of getting into it.

The good news: local SEO rewards effort more than budget. Google states plainly that there's [no way to request or pay for a better local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). This checklist walks through what actually moves your position, in the order we'd tackle it.

## How does Google rank local businesses?

Google's own help page lists three main factors:

- **Relevance:** how well your business matches what someone searched for. Complete, detailed business information helps Google match you.
- **Distance:** how far you are from the searcher, or from the location in their search.
- **Prominence:** how well known your business is, based on things like your reviews and rating and how many other websites link to you.

You can't move your office closer to every customer, so local SEO focuses on relevance and prominence.

## Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears in the map. Start here.

- **Verify the profile.** Verified businesses are more likely to appear in results.
- **Choose the right primary category,** such as "HVAC contractor" or "Plumber," and add secondary categories for other services you genuinely offer.
- **Set your service area** if you travel to customers. You can hide your street address if customers don't visit you.
- **Fill in everything:** hours (including holiday hours), phone, website, services with descriptions, and business attributes.
- **Add real photos and videos** of your team, vans and finished jobs, and keep adding new ones.
- **Use the same business name, address and phone number** everywhere online, exactly as they appear on your profile.

## Step 2: Build a steady flow of reviews

Reviews feed prominence directly, and they decide whether searchers call you once they find you. [BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found:

- **97%** of consumers read reviews for local businesses.
- **68%** require a rating of four stars or more; **31%** will only use a business rated 4.5 or higher.
- **74%** only care about reviews written in the last three months.
- **80%** are likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews.

What to do:

1. **Ask every happy customer,** ideally the same day the job is finished. A text with a direct link to your review form works best.
2. **Make it routine,** not a one-off campaign. A steady flow of recent reviews beats a burst from two years ago.
3. **Reply to every review,** positive or negative. Keep replies short, specific and polite.
4. **Never buy reviews or offer incentives for them.** It breaks Google's rules and can get your profile suspended.

## Step 3: Give every service and area its own page

Your website tells Google what you do and where. One homepage that lists everything can't rank for all of it.

- **One page per service.** "Water heater installation" and "drain cleaning" are different searches.
- **One page per main town or area you serve,** with genuinely local content: the neighborhoods you cover, typical jobs there, and reviews from local customers.
- **Clear titles and headings** that use the words customers search, such as "AC repair in Plano, TX."
- **Your name, address and phone** in the footer of every page, matching your Google Business Profile.
- **LocalBusiness schema markup,** which spells out your business details for search engines in a structured format.
- **Internal links** between related services and areas, so visitors and Google can find everything.

## Step 4: Get your business listed in the right places

Beyond Google, list your business on the directories your customers and search engines rely on: Apple Maps (through Apple Business Connect), Bing Places, Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, Nextdoor and any trade-specific directories. The most important rule is **consistency**: the same name, address, phone number and website everywhere. Mismatched details confuse search engines and AI assistants alike.

## Step 5: Earn local links

Links from other local websites are part of prominence. Good sources for home service businesses:

- Suppliers and manufacturers that list certified installers
- Local business associations and chambers of commerce
- Sponsorships of local teams, events or charities
- Local news coverage of your work or community involvement
- Neighborhood blogs and community websites

A handful of genuine local links is worth more than hundreds from unrelated directories.

## Step 6: Get ready for AI search

The way people find local businesses is shifting. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, **45%** of consumers said they'd used ChatGPT or another AI tool for local recommendations, up from 6% the year before. AI assistants pull from your website, your Google profile, review sites and directories.

To show up, keep your business details identical everywhere, answer common customer questions clearly on your website, and keep reviews coming. Our guide to [answer engine optimization](/blog/what-is-aeo-answer-engine-optimization) goes deeper.

## Step 7: Track what's working

- **Google Business Profile performance:** calls, direction requests and website clicks from your listing.
- **Google Search Console:** which searches show your site and how often people click.
- **Call and form tracking** on your website, so you know which pages bring in jobs.

Check these monthly. Local SEO is a slow compounding game, and the trend matters more than any single week.

## A 30-day local SEO plan

| Week | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Verify and fully complete your Google Business Profile; fix inconsistent business details |
| Week 2 | Start a review request routine and reply to every existing review |
| Week 3 | Create or improve service pages and your top three service-area pages |
| Week 4 | Claim key directory listings, set up tracking, and plan one local link opportunity per month |
