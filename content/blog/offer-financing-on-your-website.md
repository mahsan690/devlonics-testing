Yes, if you sell big-ticket jobs, offering financing and showing it clearly on your website can win work you'd otherwise lose to "we'll think about it." Price is the first thing customers weigh, and many homeowners say they simply don't have the cash for large projects. Financing changes the question from "can I afford $12,000?" to "can I afford this monthly payment?"

## Why financing matters now

- **Price comes first.** Jobber's [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/) found **62%** of customers weigh price first when choosing a provider, ahead of reviews (51%).
- **Many homeowners feel stretched.** In [Carrier's survey of 1,015 homeowners](https://www.carrier.com/us/en/news/carrier-finds-over-half-of-us-homeowners-plan-2026-home-improvements.html), published January 2026, **42%** of those not planning home projects said they didn't have the money, and **27%** cited economic uncertainty and inflation.
- **Value still sells.** The same survey found **82%** would consider a new HVAC system if it paid for itself in energy savings within five to ten years. Financing lets customers match payments to those savings.

## Which jobs benefit most?

- HVAC replacements and heat pump installs
- Roof replacements and storm repairs
- Remodeling, additions and outdoor living projects
- Water heater, water treatment and sewer line replacements
- Larger landscaping and hardscaping projects

For small repairs and recurring services, financing adds friction for little benefit.

## How to present financing on your website

1. **Show it next to the price.** On replacement and remodel pages, put a line under your price range: "Financing available, with options from 12 to 120 months for qualified buyers." Wording should come from your financing provider.
2. **Create a financing page** that explains how it works, who your lender is, how to apply and how long approval takes.
3. **Add it to your FAQ** ("Do you offer financing?").
4. **Mention it in quotes and follow-ups,** including the good, better, best options described in our guide to [showing prices on your website](/blog/should-you-put-prices-on-your-website).
5. **Let customers pre-qualify online** if your lender offers a soft-credit-check application link.

## The advertising rules to know

Financing ads are regulated. Under the federal Truth in Lending Act's [Regulation Z, section 1026.24](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/24/), certain "triggering terms" in an ad require extra disclosures. If your website or ad states:

- the amount or percentage of a **down payment**,
- the **number of payments** or the repayment period,
- the **amount of any payment** ("only $129 a month"), or
- the amount of a **finance charge**,

then it must also disclose the down payment, the repayment terms and the **annual percentage rate**, using that term. General statements like "financing available" don't trigger these requirements. Ask your financing provider for compliant ad language, and have someone qualified review promotional offers.

## Make it part of the sales conversation

Financing works best when it's presented as normal, early and alongside the price, not as a rescue when a customer balks. Train your team to mention it with every replacement quote, and track how many jobs close with financing.
