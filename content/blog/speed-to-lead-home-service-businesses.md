Speed to lead is the time between a customer reaching out and you replying, and in home services it often decides who gets the job. More than half of customers now expect to hear back within the hour. The good news is that most businesses can get there with better routing and a few automations, not more staff.

## How fast do customers expect a reply?

Jobber surveyed 1,050 US home service business owners in December 2025 for its [2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/). On customer expectations, it found:

- **More than 55%** of customers expect a response within the hour.
- **28%** expect an immediate reply.
- When choosing a provider, customers weigh **price (62%)**, **reviews (51%)** and **response speed (25%)**.

Price and reviews take time to change. Response speed can improve this week.

## Why replying fast wins jobs

A homeowner with a leaking water heater or a dead AC usually contacts two or three companies at once. The first one that replies with something useful, a time, a price range or a booking link, sets the frame for everyone after. Research published in *Harvard Business Review* back in 2011 already concluded that [most companies don't respond to online leads nearly fast enough](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711), and expectations have only tightened since.

## How much are you really missing?

Most owners overestimate. In Service Direct's call performance study, [owners guessed they answered 97% of calls; the measured in-house rate was 66%](https://www.abby.com/blog/are-your-home-service-calls-performing-well/). The same summary cites figures suggesting most callers don't leave voicemails and many never call back.

Check your own numbers before deciding what to fix:

1. Export last month's call log and count calls that weren't answered.
2. Look at form submissions and note the time between submission and first reply.
3. Check texts and Facebook messages the same way.

## A response system that doesn't need new staff

### Instant alerts for every lead

Every website form should arrive on a phone within seconds, not sit in an inbox someone checks at 4 p.m. We route forms to email and text, and the team member on call gets a notification they can't miss.

### An automatic first reply

An instant text or email that confirms you got the request, says when you'll call and links to your reviews buys time without feeling robotic. Keep it short and specific to the service they asked about.

### Missed-call text-back

When a call rings out, an automatic text ("Sorry we missed you, this is Mike from ABC Plumbing. Is it urgent?") turns a lost caller into a conversation. Many phone systems and field service apps offer this.

### Overflow routing and an answering service

Ring a second phone when the first is busy. Use an answering service or an [AI voice receptionist](/services/automation) for lunch hours, evenings and peak season. Service Direct's numbers suggest the gap between 66% and 99% answered is worth paying for.

### A callback routine

Agree on a rule, such as every lead gets a human reply within 15 minutes during business hours, and make one person responsible each day. Track it weekly.

## What your website can do

Your website sets expectations before anyone calls. On a [lead generation website](/services/lead-generation) we put the phone number one tap away on every page, keep forms to name, phone and what they need, and show "we reply within 15 minutes" only when the team can actually do it. If you advertise on Local Services Ads, fast answering matters even more now: Google [charges for missed calls since October 1, 2026](/blog/google-local-services-ads-missed-calls).
