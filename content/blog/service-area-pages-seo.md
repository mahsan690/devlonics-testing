Service area pages are pages on your website for the towns you serve, built to show up when someone searches "AC repair Plano" or "roof replacement Frisco." Done well, they're among the best local SEO assets a service business has. Done badly, as near-identical copies with the city name swapped, they're what Google calls doorway abuse.

## Why service area pages matter

Most home service businesses work in towns where they don't have an office. Your Google Business Profile carries a lot of weight in the map results, but it's tied to one location. A strong page for each key town gives Google, and the people searching, a reason to choose you there.

## What does Google consider spam?

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) define doorway abuse as "when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries," and give this example: "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page."

The same policies warn against scaled content abuse: lots of pages created mainly to game rankings rather than help people. Fifty city pages generated from one template with the name swapped fit both descriptions.

## What makes an area page genuinely useful?

Write each page so someone from that town would find something they couldn't get from your homepage:

- **Jobs you've done there:** short project summaries, with before and after photos.
- **Local conditions:** older housing stock, hard water, clay soil, HOA rules, storm patterns, permit quirks.
- **Reviews from customers in that town.**
- **Response times and coverage:** "We typically reach central Plano within 45 minutes."
- **Which services are most requested locally,** linking to your main service pages.
- **Neighborhoods and landmarks** you actually serve, used naturally.
- **A clear call to action** with your number and quote form.

## A simple area page structure

1. **H1:** "[Service] in [Town], [State]"
2. **Opening answer:** two or three sentences on what you do there and how fast you can get there.
3. **Services in [Town]:** links to your main service pages.
4. **Recent jobs in [Town]:** photos and short write-ups.
5. **What [Town] homeowners should know:** the local conditions section.
6. **Reviews** from local customers.
7. **FAQ** specific to that area.
8. **Call to action.**

## How many pages should you build?

Start with the five to ten towns that bring your most valuable work, and add more only when you have real material: jobs, reviews and photos from that area. A few strong pages beat dozens of thin ones.

## Make the rest of your local SEO match

- **Google Business Profile:** set a service area that reflects where you actually work. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say it "shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time" from your base in most cases.
- **Consistent details:** the towns on your website should match your profile and directory listings.
- **Internal links:** link area pages from your service pages and footer so they're easy to find.
