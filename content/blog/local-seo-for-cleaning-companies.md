Local SEO for a cleaning company is the work of showing up when people nearby search "house cleaning near me," "maid service" or "move-out cleaning," and getting them to book you rather than the next listing. Most cleaning clients choose from the map results, so your Google Business Profile comes first, followed by reviews and a website with a page for each kind of clean.

Google ranks local results on [relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). Here's how a cleaning business improves each.

## 1. Set up your Google Business Profile for cleaning

- **Primary category:** For a residential cleaning company, usually "House cleaning service." Google says to choose [the most specific category](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669) and warns not to add a category for every service. Add commercial or carpet cleaning only if you genuinely do them.
- **Services:** List every type: standard cleaning, deep cleaning, move-in and move-out, recurring weekly or biweekly, post-construction, office cleaning.
- **Service areas:** Most cleaning companies go to clients, so [remove your address and set service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481): up to 20 cities or postal codes, within about a 2-hour drive. In practice, choose the areas you can reach without losing money on travel.
- **Photos:** Your team, your branded supplies and before-and-after shots (with the client's permission).
- **Hours:** When you take bookings and when you clean.

## 2. Make reviews your engine

Cleaning is a trust purchase: clients are letting someone into their home, often while they're out. [BrightLocal's 2026 survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found:

- **97%** of consumers read reviews for local businesses.
- **74%** only care about reviews from the last three months.
- **47%** won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.

Ask after the first clean and occasionally afterwards for recurring clients. Our [review request templates](/blog/review-request-templates) include one for cleaning visits, and our guide on [responding to negative reviews](/blog/how-to-respond-to-negative-reviews) covers the rest.

## 3. Build a page for each kind of clean

| Page | Who searches for it |
| --- | --- |
| Standard or regular cleaning | Busy households wanting ongoing help |
| Deep cleaning | First-time clients, spring cleaning, before events |
| Move-in and move-out cleaning | Tenants, landlords, realtors |
| Recurring service (weekly, biweekly) | Your most valuable clients |
| Office or commercial cleaning | Small offices, clinics, shops |
| Each main town or neighborhood | Clients searching locally |

On each page, explain what's included, show starting prices or ranges, add reviews from that kind of clean and put a booking button at the top. Our guide on [showing prices on your website](/blog/should-you-put-prices-on-your-website) helps with the pricing decision.

## 4. Use Nextdoor and neighborhood word of mouth

Cleaning spreads street by street. A Nextdoor business page is [free to claim](https://www.constantcontact.com/blog/nextdoor-advertising/), and recommendations from neighbors drive visibility there: a business doesn't appear in Nextdoor search until it has three. Ask your happiest clients to recommend you.

## 5. Keep your details identical everywhere

Your name, phone number and website should match exactly on Google, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook and Nextdoor. Inconsistent details confuse search engines and AI assistants that recommend local businesses.

## 6. Turn one-off cleans into recurring clients

Local SEO brings the first booking. Recurring service is where cleaning businesses make their money. Our guide to [winning recurring cleaning clients](/blog/cleaning-business-marketing-recurring-clients) covers the follow-up that turns a deep clean into a biweekly client.

## 7. Track what works

- Calls, website clicks and direction requests from your Google profile
- Booking requests by website page
- How many first-time clients become recurring

Check monthly and look at the trend.
